The Harrow Canal was dug between 1791 and 1796 to carry slate from the upland quarries to the river port at Callow. Its forty-two locks climb ninety metres in under eleven kilometres, which made it one of the steepest working canals of its time. Horse-drawn narrow boats moved about twelve tonnes per trip, and a full ascent took most of a day. Traffic peaked in the 1840s, when more than three hundred boats a month passed the summit lock. The railway reached Callow in 1868 and halved that number within a decade, and the last commercial cargo, a load of roofing slate, passed in 1931. After decades of neglect, volunteers began clearing the lower pound in 1974. Today the restored flight is a walking route, and three of the original lock-keepers' cottages house a small museum about the slate trade.
