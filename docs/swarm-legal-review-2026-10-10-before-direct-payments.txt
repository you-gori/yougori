YOUGORI SWARM MINING LEGAL MEMORANDUM

To: Yougori project maintainer
From: Codex research draft for the maintainer and qualified legal counsel
Date: 10 October 2026
Re: Responsible use rules, open source rights, liability and the hosted USDC bounty service

This is an implementation-focused legal risk assessment and drafting review. It
is not a signed lawyer's opinion, a finding of compliance in every country, or a
claim of attorney-client privilege. The text follows the Background, Analysis
and Conclusion structure of the supplied Legal Memorandum template. The
maintainer requested text instead of a DOCX after the template's required
document runtime was found unavailable.

Recommendation: continue controlled, authorized, local-source testing with the
recorded rules implemented here. Do not treat these rules as clearance for a
worldwide paid launch. Before taking or settling real bounty funds, identify
the actual operator and obtain jurisdiction-specific advice on the financial
flow, privacy arrangements and service terms. Open source distribution and
operation of a hosted service have different responsibilities.

BACKGROUND

1. Instructions and confirmed facts

The maintainer confirmed that Yougori is individually built, has no registered
legal company name, is open source, and is intended for worldwide users. Bounty
offers come from Yougori's hosted platform. No governing law, legal operator
name, operator address, insurance, financial authorization, or business-only
restriction has been supplied. This memorandum does not invent those facts.
An individual can be the relevant operator; incorporation is not asserted to
be universally mandatory. The operator's personal exposure needs local advice.

The public Yougori source is offered under AGPL-3.0-only, with separately
licensed components. The bounty coordination backend is in the private website
checkout. Calling the local client open source does not establish that the
private service is open source. The hosted agent channel's privacy is an access
policy, not an assurance that administrators cannot inspect a computer.

The current pilot works on company-authorized local source snapshots. A
participant prepares a Hugging Face model, receives an offer, reviews its exact
scope, source revision, deadlines and reward, and explicitly accepts before
investigation. The model API is private. OpenCode's configured tools are limited
to reading the source and managed tasks. Company-declared checks run in a
separate offline test sandbox. This pilot does not authorize live-target tests.

The platform stores source, structured attempts, evidence, reports, agent
coordination, private human-agent messages, acceptance evidence and financial
records. Accepted workers receive source on participant-controlled computers.
Platform encryption protects stored records at rest; the service controls its
keys. Delivered local copies cannot reliably be recalled. An administrator can
inspect files, memory or credentials. Confidentiality therefore needs both
accurate notices and enforceable obligations between actual parties.

Rewards use third-party USDC, not a Yougori-issued cryptocurrency. Confirmed
account credit funds a reserve. After the review and appeal process, a winning
award credits account earnings. A withdrawal is a separate pending request
requiring operator settlement. An earnings credit is not an on-chain payment.
Test funding is explicitly simulated and unavailable in production. Database
balance reservations do not establish legal escrow or segregated custody.

Public checkout: LICENSE, COPYING, NOTICE, COMMERCIAL_LICENSE.txt,
docs/licensing.txt, docs/swarm-mining.md, cli/src/bounty.rs,
src-tauri/src/swarm/mod.rs, guest.rs, runner.rs, worker-rules.md, AGENTS.md,

Private website checkout: server/swarm/index.mjs, schema.mjs, validation.mjs,
rules.mjs and service-rules.json; the company wizard, scope editor, participant
acceptance portal and public rules page; privacy/security notices; release
packaging; Swarm server, runtime-contract and browser tests. The existing model
library's stored-file delivery was also checked where the privacy notice had
an outdated statement about publisher-hosted downloads.

The official legal and regulator sources linked in the analysis were consulted
on the memorandum date. Some EUR-Lex links redirected or presented a browser
challenge; official regulator rulebooks and Commission pages were used where
available. National transpositions, court practice, all countries' laws, the
operator's actual regulatory status and model-by-model licenses have not been
independently audited. This limitation affects any launch decision.

3. Changes made in this review

The service now exposes responsible use rules at /api/swarm/rules and the
website displays them at /swarm/rules, including while the pilot is disabled.
There are eleven sections covering eligibility, authorization, malicious use,
integrity, open source rights, confidentiality, access, processing permissions,
responsibilities, rewards and stopping when authorization ends.

Publication and new scope versions require the publisher to affirm the exact
rule version and SHA-256 digest as well as source authority, local delivery and
reward obligations. Participants must explicitly accept the exact rules in
their offer. The full rule document is included in the immutable bounty terms
and therefore in the bounty terms digest. The server refuses missing, false,
stale or mismatched rule acceptance. A bypassed website checkbox does not
bypass that server requirement.

Acceptance evidence records the account, role, bounty version or membership,
rule version, digest, encrypted rule document and timestamp. Idempotent retries
do not create duplicate acceptances. Database triggers prevent ordinary edits
or deletion of these records. An administrator with database control is still
technically capable of overriding the database; no absolute tamper-proof or
cryptographic identity guarantee is claimed.

Schema migration adds the acceptance table without rewriting old terms or
fabricating past consent. An old published offer lacking rules cannot gain a
new acceptance until republished with rules. Existing receipts and reward
obligations are not silently replaced. Scripted CLI acceptance requires the
new --accept-rules option, alongside exact bounty terms and --yes. Interactive
acceptance displays the full attached rules before the final choice.

Agent operating instructions now prohibit malicious use, out-of-scope testing,
safeguard bypasses, impersonation and evidence tampering. These instructions
supplement the existing server permissions and isolated execution; they do not
make a modified model or participant computer trustworthy.

The website notices now describe Swarm storage and administrator access,
conditional USDC rewards and private source handling. An unrelated inaccurate
privacy sentence saying library downloads connect to publishers was corrected
to describe the existing Yougori stored-file delivery.

Current top-level project, licensing and installer notices were corrected so
they no longer represent that an LLC exists. The original AGPL text and
third-party licenses remain intact. Historical compliance inventories and
previously generated packages were not rewritten; they need review before
reuse as evidence for a new release. These edits do not resolve who legally
owns every contribution or create an executed commercial agreement.

ANALYSIS

4. Worldwide operation and the identity of the operator

"Every country" is an intended audience, not a governing law. Applicable rules
can depend on the operator, users, publisher, target, payment provider and data
locations. A chosen contract law would not automatically displace mandatory
rules. EU consumer conflict-of-law rules preserve relevant mandatory consumer
protection in qualifying cases. The users here are not contractually limited
to businesses, so the review cannot assume that consumer protections never
apply. Authority: Rome I Regulation, Article 6, official text:
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A32008R0593

Practical recommendation: identify the person actually operating the service,
the contact and service address, and the legal jurisdiction of that operation.
Publish whatever disclosures applicable law requires. Keep the brand Yougori,
but do not describe it as an incorporated limited-liability entity without
evidence. Whether incorporation, business registration or a representative is
needed depends on the chosen activity and locations. Decide initially supported
paid markets from legal advice rather than a worldwide marketing claim.

No legal operator name or address has been inserted into public terms. The
existing hi@yougori.com contact is provided for reporting, but an email address
alone is not a complete operator identification or regulatory contact regime.

5. Open source and the request to prevent editing

The existing LICENSE explicitly permits modification and redistribution under
AGPL-3.0-only. COPYING contains the unmodified license. A blanket "do not edit
Yougori" restriction would conflict with the rights this project already
grants. A policy cannot make every downstream fork obey Yougori's hosted
service controls or give Yougori rights it does not own.

The implemented distinction is appropriate: lawful modification remains
permitted under the software license, while service users must not bypass
authentication, target scope, isolation, budgets or stop controls, impersonate
agents, or falsify canonical records. Legitimate edits to a test copy are
allowed when documented; they do not rewrite the accepted source revision.
The host may enforce access requirements through its API. It should not claim
that a lawful software fork itself automatically breaches these service rules.

AGPL notices and applicable corresponding-source obligations remain. A private
checkout does not prove a proprietary backend is exempt from AGPL: counsel
must inspect its provenance, copied or linked code and actual interaction with
covered works. Separate communication is relevant but not a conclusive legal
classification. The server must not simply incorporate AGPL-covered code and
assume that naming it a private service removes license obligations.

Model and tool licenses also matter. A Hugging Face repository or "unrestricted"
label is not evidence of permission for every use. The rules require lawful
access and applicable license compliance. Preserve notices for the exact
OpenCode version and other distributed components; the current upstream
OpenCode license is MIT, but release-specific provenance still needs recording.
Authority for the upstream license:
https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/blob/dev/LICENSE

6. Authorization and computer misuse

The EU attacks-against-information-systems framework concerns unauthorized
conduct and does not supply a platform-wide permission to test third parties.
Ownership or valid authorization must cover the actual code and test. A
publisher's assertion is useful evidence but is not verification of authority.
Authority: Directive 2013/40/EU, including its authorization definition:
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2013/40/oj/eng

Other countries apply their own computer misuse rules. For example, the UK
Computer Misuse Act addresses unauthorized access. US Department of Justice
policy protects qualifying good-faith security research from its CFAA charging
policy, but it is not a universal immunity or permission for extortion. It does
not settle other countries' laws or private claims. Authorities:
https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1990/18?timeline=false
https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-new-policy-charging-cases-under-computer-fraud-and-abuse-act

The local-source-only pilot is a sensible risk boundary. Keep live production,
external services, real users, host/LAN systems and unrelated participants
outside scope. Require written source authority and exact permitted tests.
Check source rights, corporate representation and dependent third-party rights
before enrolling confidential projects. A paid production publisher should be
reviewed as an actual counterparty, not treated as verified because it supplied
an organization name.

A future publisher safe-harbor clause can state that the publisher authorizes
the described research and will not assert its own claims against compliant
researchers for that authorized conduct. It must identify the publisher, exact
scope, permitted acts, confidentiality and disclosure process. It cannot bind
third-party owners, regulators or prosecutors. No universal safe harbor has
been inserted into the current rules.

Keep evidence of scope versions, acceptance, source hashes, pause/revocation
and completed checks. On unclear or accidental out-of-scope access, pause,
minimize retained sensitive data and contact the authorized reviewer privately.
Do not continue exploring merely to establish a stronger report.

7. Liability and warranty wording

The requested blanket statement "we are not responsible" is not a reliable
legal solution. Standard terms can be unenforceable where unfair or contrary
to mandatory protections. EU consumer rules require intelligible terms and do
not bind consumers to unfair standard provisions. Authority:
https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/consumer-contract-law/unfair-contract-terms-directive_en

The implemented clause allocates the participant's permissions, lawful
instructions, uploads and local computer to that participant. It explains that
Yougori provides coordination and tooling and does not certify security or
guarantee availability, findings, earnings or payment timing. The exclusion
for losses caused by unauthorized user conduct is expressly limited by
applicable law. It preserves Yougori's own legal obligations and liability
that cannot lawfully be excluded.

This is a drafting choice, not an assertion that the same clause is enforceable
everywhere. The text does not impose a zero liability cap, blanket indemnity,
mandatory arbitration, waiver of every claim or forfeiture of all balances.
Any future business liability cap should be tied to the actual paid service,
counterparty, fees, insurance, foreseeable losses and mandatory carve-outs
after local advice. A disclaimer does not protect deliberate wrongdoing or
replace reasonable security and response procedures.

The individual operator must not assume that project branding creates limited
liability. Investigate appropriate structure and insurance before entering
commercial source-handling and payment arrangements. Software can itself
engage product obligations in applicable circumstances. The Commission states
that the new EU Product Liability Directive must be transposed by 9 December
2026; national implementation and the relevant product/date need checking.
Authority:
https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/single-market/goods/free-movement-sectors/liability-defective-products_en

8. USDC funding and payments

This is the highest unresolved launch issue. The actual flow involves receipt
of USDC by an operator wallet, internal user credits, earmarked bounty reserves,
winner earnings and operator-settled withdrawals. Neither being open source,
having no proprietary coin, describing a balance as credit, nor operating
manually establishes an exemption from financial rules.

Inference: custody, transfer, payment-service or related obligations may be
engaged, depending on control of funds and keys, contractual rights, operator
location and customers. This memorandum does not determine that Yougori is
already an authorized CASP, or conclusively classify it as one. MiCA Article 59
restricts provision of relevant crypto-asset services in the Union to authorized
or otherwise eligible entities. Authority:
https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-59-authorisation

If custody rules apply, an internal reserve table is not sufficient proof of
the required segregation or client protection. MiCA Article 75 addresses
custody agreements, custody policies, recordkeeping, return of assets,
segregation and liability for attributable losses. The rules must not call the
current implementation regulated escrow, insured custody or bankruptcy-safe
funds without establishing those arrangements. Authority:
https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-75-providing-custody-and

Electronic-money-token services can also raise payment-services issues. The
EBA's 2026 opinion addresses supervision after the MiCA/PSD2 transition period
ended on 2 March 2026. Do not assume an old transition announcement permits
present unlicensed operation; the subset of activities, conditions and current
national treatment need professional assessment. Authority:
https://eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-02/3b8b6f18-ca26-4ce1-83eb-d060276f3301/Opinion%20on%20the%20end%20of%20the%20NAL%20transition%20period.pdf

Where applicable, AML and transfer-information requirements require a real
operational process. Wallet ownership signatures alone are not customer due
diligence. The EBA travel-rule guidance describes information accompanying
covered crypto-asset transfers. Authority:
https://www.eba.europa.eu/activities/single-rulebook/regulatory-activities/anti-money-laundering-and-countering-financing-terrorism/guidelines-information-requirements-relation-transfers-funds-and-certain-crypto-assets-transfers

US availability raises separate questions. FinCEN's CVC guidance distinguishes
software and business activities using or accepting/transmitting value. Do not
treat publishing code as identical to operating this treasury and withdrawal
service. Federal and relevant state requirements need assessment before
supporting US money flows. Authority:
https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf

Sanctions applicability and counterparties also need screening appropriate to
the actual operator and transaction nexus. A user checkbox does not discharge
the operator's own applicable sanctions duties. OFAC's virtual-currency guidance
is one relevant US source, not a universal statement that every operator has
the same US obligations. Authority:
https://ofac.treasury.gov/system/files/126/virtual_currency_guidance_brochure.pdf

Recommendation before live funds: obtain a written classification of the exact
funds flow; select supported markets; use an appropriately authorized provider
where needed; document treasury ownership, client rights, segregation,
reconciliation, refunds, fees, withdrawal timing and failure handling; complete
required identity, AML and sanctions processes. Continue simulated funding
until those questions are resolved. No real transfer or production deployment
was made during this review.

9. Reward contracts and fair review

The current rules correctly distinguish an attempted investigation, candidate,
submitted report, verified qualifying report, award and settled payment. The
earliest complete server-received report subsequently verified under the
accepted criteria wins. Automatic private submission and human-reviewed
submission have different receipt timing; the participant chooses explicitly.
There is no automatic split among contributing agents.

Keep qualifying criteria, exclusions, root-cause duplicate treatment, evidence
requirements, deadlines, review/appeal windows and payout descriptions clear
before acceptance. Scope changes require fresh acceptance and preserve older
report versions. Do not retroactively shorten prior obligations or confiscate
earned rights solely because an account is restricted.

The independent appeal reviewer is a technical separation of account roles,
not proof of institutional independence, a legally binding arbitration forum,
or a guaranteed impartial adjudicator. Identify the actual reviewer, conflict
policy and escalation route in a production program. Internal appeals cannot
remove non-waivable external remedies. Avoid marketing "mine guaranteed USDC"
or implying that participation creates investment returns. Here, "Swarm
Mining" is a name for security bounty work, not blockchain consensus mining.

10. Source intellectual property and confidentiality

Publishers should attest to authority for code, documentation, fixtures and
dependent materials and provide a limited license for the accepted research.
The implemented rules permit operation, delivery, analysis, authorized agent
coordination, review and disputes; they preserve existing ownership and do not
make confidential source publicly redistributable or authorize model training.

Participant reports and evidence need permission for private processing and
publisher verification/remediation. An assignment of inventions, exclusive
rights or a broad publication license is not imposed here. If a particular
publisher needs different report rights, use clear bounty-specific terms and
review them before acceptance. Reward payment does not automatically resolve
copyright ownership in every jurisdiction.

For trade-secret material, use approved enrollment, actual participant
identity and confidentiality agreements where appropriate, least-necessary
snapshots, synthetic data and prompt revocation. Do not promise that local
administrators cannot copy delivered source. Confidentiality does not create
cryptographic recall. Protect disclosures legally required by regulators,
courts or protected reporting; avoid a clause forbidding all reports of misuse.

11. Privacy and international local workers

Inference from the actual operation: Yougori is likely to determine purposes
for account administration, abuse controls, acceptance evidence and rewards.
It may process some publisher project data on the publisher's instructions.
Participant operators may have additional roles. Roles must follow the facts,
not simply a clause calling everyone a processor. Processor arrangements and
authorized subprocessors need concrete obligations. Authority: EDPB Guidelines
07/2020, especially the controller/processor analysis and contract requirements:
https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2023-10/EDPB_guidelines_202007_controllerprocessor_final_en.pdf

Serving EU users can bring an operator outside the EU within GDPR scope in
qualifying circumstances. Physical server location or open source status is
not the only criterion. The operator's actual establishment, offering and
processing need assessment; representative requirements may also need review.
Authority: EDPB territorial-scope guidance:
https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-32018-on-the-territorial-scope-of-the-gdpr-article-3-version-adopted_en

The privacy program needs identified controllers, lawful bases, recipients,
retention, data-subject rights, security and breach procedures, and valid
international-transfer arrangements where required. Consent to service rules
is not blanket GDPR consent or a substitute for a lawful basis. The GDPR
processor, security, breach and international-transfer provisions are relevant
to the actual data flows. Official text:
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN-DE/TXT/?from=EN&uri=CELEX%3A32016R0679

Current gaps from the code review: there is no complete Swarm retention job,
dedicated export/deletion workflow, project-specific DPA, participant-operator
subprocessor registry or geographic source-delivery restriction. Accounts and
bot identifiers can identify people indirectly. Do not assume synthetic project
fixtures eliminate personal data in messages, logs, evidence or billing.

Recommendation: first minimize data and prohibit production secrets. Before
confidential live projects, map each recipient and local-worker location,
determine roles, obtain necessary agreements and transfer safeguards, define
retention by record type and implement deletion/export with documented legal
holds. Database immutability is an audit mechanism, not a legal right to retain
personal data forever. A controlled, accountable deletion process may need to
handle trigger-protected records when retention no longer has a lawful basis.
The new notice candidly describes current storage; it does not claim these
missing operational processes already exist.

12. AI transparency and cybersecurity product obligations

Local inference and open source do not automatically remove AI obligations.
Determine whether Yougori is acting as a provider, deployer or another actor
for the actual system and models. The Commission's current AI Act timeline
states that general application began on 2 August 2026, with separate phased
dates and 2026 Omnibus changes for certain high-risk systems. Do not rely on
an older timeline or assume every security tool is automatically high-risk.
Authority:
https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai

Practical recommendation: label agent messages and findings as AI-generated;
retain human review of awards and authority; give users scope/stop controls;
record foreseeable misuse and limitations. Classify intended uses before
adding autonomous live-target execution, critical-infrastructure use or other
materially different deployments. This is not a conformity assessment.

The Commission explains that commercially supplied open source can fall
within the Cyber Resilience Act. Its main obligations apply from 11 December
2027 and reporting obligations from 11 September 2026. The product/activity
classification and the actual operator's role need checking now rather than
assuming every obligation is deferred. Authorities:
https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-open-source
https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act

Maintain component provenance, security updates, vulnerability handling and
an incident escalation owner. The service email and software prompts alone
are not a complete legally required vulnerability-reporting process.

13. Hosted content and misuse response

If the hosted service qualifies as an intermediary/hosting service under the
DSA, relevant duties can include mechanisms for reporting illegal content,
action on notices and reasons for certain restrictions. Qualification,
territorial scope and applicable exemptions need assessment. Merely calling
the system a bot-only room does not decide that classification, and a liability
disclaimer does not create intermediary immunity. Authorities:
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065
https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/dsa-transparency-database-questions-and-answers

The implemented policy provides a private reporting contact, permits lawful
service restrictions and preserves valid reward obligations. Complete the
operational process: receive reports, preserve necessary evidence, assess
authority, pause scoped access, decide proportionate restrictions, communicate
reasons where required and maintain an appeal or correction route. Develop a
restricted incident workflow rather than publishing private evidence in a
public issue tracker. No claim is made that an automated classifier currently
detects every malicious bounty or that every report email will be monitored
within a particular promised period.

CONCLUSION

14. Decision and actions

The maintainer can adopt these locally implemented responsible use rules for
the authorized source-only pilot. They make the user's obligations explicit,
preserve AGPL rights, improve contract evidence and avoid a misleading blanket
liability waiver. They cannot guarantee nobody will misuse an open-source fork,
prove publisher ownership, make private code inaccessible to administrators,
or authorize a worldwide financial service.

Before a paid public launch, the maintainer should resolve these matters with
qualified counsel and, where applicable, a regulated payment partner:

1) Identify the actual legal operator, service disclosures and initial supported
   paid markets. Determine applicable law from real facts; do not invent an LLC.
2) Obtain the financial-services classification and required authorizations or
   partner arrangement for deposits, reserves, earnings and withdrawals.
3) Complete authority/counterparty review, review independence, refund and
   withdrawal terms and documented financial reconciliation.
4) Complete privacy roles, DPA/subprocessor and transfer arrangements, retention,
   export/deletion and incident handling for confidential source and user data.
5) Check current AI/CRA/product duties and third-party/model license provenance.
6) Review localized service terms, consumer treatment, liability, complaints,
   source confidentiality and any publisher safe-harbor wording.

These are launch recommendations, not implemented features or completed legal
approvals. The operator decides the supported markets after obtaining advice.
If those requirements cannot yet be met, the fallback is a closed controlled
pilot with synthetic authorized source and simulated rewards, not a disclaimer
asserting every activity is legal everywhere.

The current changes have not been committed, pushed, deployed or installed into
the running desktop application. No live funds were sent. The prior instruction
to keep Swarm changes local remains in force.

15. Implemented user-facing wording

The complete canonical wording is in docs/swarm-responsible-use.txt and the
private service's service-rules.json, version 2026-10-10.1. It is shown in the
offer before acceptance and at /swarm/rules. The responsibility clause reads:

"You are responsible for your permissions, lawful instructions, uploads, local
computer, and actions you direct. Review AI findings: models can be wrong, and
an unsuccessful test does not prove a project is safe. Yougori supplies
coordination and tooling; it does not certify security or guarantee availability,
a correct finding, earnings, or payment timing. To the extent permitted by
applicable law, no warranty of those outcomes is given and Yougori is not
responsible for losses caused by a user's unauthorized conduct. This does not
exclude Yougori's own legal obligations or liability that cannot lawfully be
excluded, including applicable consumer, data protection, fraud, intent, gross
negligence, or personal injury protections. No blanket waiver, automatic
indemnity, or arbitrary monetary liability cap is imposed by these rules."

The legal review is a draft for jurisdiction-specific validation, not a promise
that clicking a checkbox removes anyone's statutory rights or obligations.
